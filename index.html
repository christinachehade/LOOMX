How We Work

From idea to working product.

Every project starts by understanding the clinical problem, research, users, and opportunity. From there, we shape the right product, design it, build it, and help bring it into the real world.

  1. 01

    Understand

    We learn the medicine, research, users, workflow, and problem behind the idea.

  2. 02

    Shape

    We define what the product should become, who it is for, what belongs in the first version, and what success looks like.

  3. 03

    Design

    We translate the clinical or research concept into clear workflows, prototypes, and an intuitive product experience — then test and refine it before development.

  4. 04

    Build

    We engineer the real product — web, mobile, clinical platforms, integrations, infrastructure, AI capabilities where useful, and everything required to make it work.

  5. 05

    Launch & Evolve

    We help prepare the product for pilots, deployment, real users, and continued iteration — whether the goal is clinical use, research validation, or commercialization.

Already further along? We can enter at any stage; from an early idea or grant proposal to an existing prototype or product that needs to be redesigned, rebuilt, or taken to launch.

Built for Healthcare

The details matter.

Healthcare products have constraints ordinary software doesn't. We account for them from the beginning.

  • Privacy & security

    HIPAA, GDPR, PIPEDA and appropriate data-handling requirements considered based on the product and market.

  • Interoperability

    EHR integrations, APIs, FHIR/HL7 and existing healthcare infrastructure where required.

  • Accessibility

    Inclusive, accessible experiences designed for real patients and clinical users.

  • AI where it belongs

    AI and automation integrated when they meaningfully improve the product; not added for the sake of saying it's AI-powered.

FAQ

Questions we hear before every kickoff.

I have an idea, but nothing designed yet. Is it too early to talk to you? +

Not at all. Many projects begin with a clinical problem, research finding, or idea rather than a finished product specification. Helping shape what should be built is part of our work.

I'm a physician, not a technology founder. Can you lead the product side? +

Yes. You don't need to arrive knowing how to scope software, write technical requirements, or manage a development team. You bring the clinical expertise. We help turn it into the product.

Can you work on grant-funded research? +

Yes. We can work within grant scopes and milestones to design and build prototypes, research tools, digital interventions, pilot-ready software, and other digital deliverables.

Can you turn existing research into an app or platform? +

Yes. We work with research teams to translate validated methods, interventions, assessments, and research frameworks into usable digital products while preserving the intent of the underlying work.

Can you build something we intend to commercialize? +

Yes. We can take a product from early concept through MVP, pilot, production, and continued development with commercialization in mind.

Can you work with universities or technology-transfer teams? +

Yes. We can work alongside principal investigators, research offices, and technology-transfer teams, and shape the work around institutional requirements for IP, licensing, and commercialization.

Who owns the product and IP? +

You do. The product, code, designs, and documentation are yours, and ownership terms are agreed in writing before work begins. Where a university or institution holds rights to the underlying research, we work within those arrangements.

Can you integrate with EHRs and existing healthcare systems? +

Yes. We integrate with EHR and practice management platforms through their existing APIs and standards such as FHIR and HL7, along with scheduling, billing, and communication tools. If a system doesn't expose an open API, we'll tell you upfront.

Can our product include AI? +

Yes, when it meaningfully improves the product. We design and build AI features and automation where they add real value, with clear boundaries on where clinical judgment stays in charge and how patient data is handled.

What happens after launch? +

Launch is not necessarily the end of the engagement. We can continue to support, maintain, and evolve the product based on real-world use — from fixing issues and refining the experience to adding features, improving workflows, and preparing for broader rollout or commercialization.

For research projects, that can also mean supporting pilot iterations, study feedback, or the transition from a research prototype to a production-ready product.
